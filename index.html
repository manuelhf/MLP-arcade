Learning rate: 0.20

Each step: w ← w − lr × slope

What to notice

The step is lr × slope, so steps shrink on their own as the slope flattens near the bottom. A big learning rate overshoots and bounces to the other side of the valley, or flies off the map. A tiny one is safe but slow. On a plateau the slope is almost zero, so steps shrink even if the valley is far away.

To see bouncing: gentle bowl with lr 1.8 (zig-zag that slowly settles), lr 2.0 (bounces forever), lr 2.1 (bounces wider and wider). Narrow canyon with lr 0.30 does the same on a much steeper valley.

What to notice

With a linear activation, adding straight lines gives a straight line, no matter how many neurons you have. ReLU gives kinks, so three neurons buy you up to three bends. Sigmoid and tanh give smooth steps; a bump is one step up minus one step down.

Zero hidden neurons is a single straight line. Each ReLU neuron adds one fold; XOR needs two, the circle needs about three, the spiral wants many. If training stalls, reset: a bad start can leave a ReLU dead forever.

What to notice

Training error only ever goes down. The hidden-set error goes down, then at some point turns around: the network stops learning the curve and starts memorizing the noise. Winning means stopping before that, or using a network too small to memorize.
